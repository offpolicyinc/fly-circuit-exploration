Finding 2 of 8 · a hole in the published pathway

The turn command is not delivered to the legs. It is reprocessed first.

The steering neurons compare the fly's heading with its goal and output a left-or-right turn signal. The published account sends that signal straight to a descending neuron that drives turning. In the whole-brain wiring that direct connection is a rounding error: 1.5 % of the descending neuron's input. Most of the turn signal goes to a set of interneurons, several inhibitory, that fan out to five different descending neurons. And the separately described "steering hierarchy" in that same region receives nothing from the steering neurons at all. There is a computation between the compass and the legs that no one has recorded from.

Background

Steering neurons
PFL3 cells (24) each hold a copy of the compass heading and a copy of the goal direction and fire more when the two disagree in a particular direction. The left population signals "turn one way", the right population "turn the other way" (Mussells Pires 2024; Westeinde 2024). PFL2 cells (12) signal how large the error is, without a side.
Descending neurons
The ~1,300 neurons that carry commands from brain to the ventral nerve cord, which runs the legs. DNa02 is the one the steering papers name: activating it turns the fly, and PFL3 connects to it.
Lateral accessory lobe (LAL)
The region between the compass and the descending neurons. Feng et al. 2024 described a steering hierarchy there: LAL013 and DNa03 at the top, DNa11 below, producing turns of all sizes. How the compass reaches it was left open.
What Hulse et al. 2021 already noticed
In the hemibrain, PFL3 targets LAL interneurons as well as DNs, and the authors remarked that "the LAL interneuron network plays a crucial role in shaping any rotational or translational signals generated by the CX." The steering papers that followed did not take this up.
Data
MaleCNS 2026 whole-brain connectome, every synapse from every PFL cell traced one and two hops toward descending neurons. New here: the shares of each target's input, the strict contralaterality, the second-hop convergence, and the absence of any PFL input to Feng's hierarchy.

Why it matters

  • "Goal minus heading → turn" hides a computation. The turn signal passes through a layer that can invert sign (inhibitory interneurons), change gain, and split one command into five descending channels. Whatever the legs receive is not a copy of PFL3.
  • Push-pull is the simplest reading. Left PFL3 excites right DNa02 directly and, through inhibitory LAL121, suppresses right DNa03. A single error signal becomes "drive this, brake that", which is how a controller gets fast, stable turns.
  • Two literatures do not connect. The compass-steering papers end at DNa02; Feng's steering hierarchy starts at LAL013/DNa03 and receives no PFL input. The interneurons above (LAL121, LAL014, LAL122 → DNa03) are the only structural link between them, and none has been recorded.

What would settle it

  • Image LAL121 and LAL126 during compass-driven corrective turns (the Green 2019 bump-shift assay); predict activity phase-locked to error sign, contralateral to the active PFL3 side.
  • Activate left PFL3 and record DNa02 and DNa03 on the right: the push-pull reading predicts opposite-sign responses.
  • Silence LAL121 alone: if steering degrades, the direct PFL3 → DNa02 line is not sufficient.
