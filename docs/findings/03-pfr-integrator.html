Finding 3 of 8 · the most important negative

The fly's path integrator is not in the wiring as excitatory circuitry. One measurement decides whether it is there at all.

Flies provably add up their movements: they walk a loop in the dark and return to where food was, and silencing a population called PFR abolishes their memory of distance. So PFR is the prime suspect for the integrator. Its wiring has the right ingredients, a travel-direction input and mutual excitation between its two subtypes, but a simulation with the measured wiring shows PFR only relays travel direction; it never accumulates. Integration becomes possible only if PFR's broad self-connections are inhibitory. PFR_a's transmitter is unresolved, so one immunostain settles whether the fly's path integrator has been found.

Background

Path integration
Keeping a running sum of every step's direction and length, so the animal always knows the straight-line vector back to the start. Ants and bees do it over hundreds of metres; flies do it over centimetres in the dark (Kim & Dickinson 2017; Behbahani 2021; Titova 2023).
What is already known in the fly
The instantaneous inputs are solved: PFNd and PFNv encode the fly's velocity, and hΔB combines them with heading to give travel direction in world coordinates (Lu 2022; Lyu 2022). What no one has found is the neuron that adds those up over time.
PFR
44 cells in two subtypes (PFR_a, PFR_b) linking the compass bridge, the fan-shaped body and a small output region. Recent preprints show PFR activity tracks walking direction and drifts when the fly stops (Flores-Valle 2025), and that silencing PFR removes a learned distance memory (D'Atri 2025). Neither paper claims PFR integrates.
How an integrator has to be wired
A population integrates a vector input only if its recurrent connections give the "which direction" pattern of activity a gain of about 1 (so it persists and accumulates) while keeping the "everything on" pattern below 1 (so it does not saturate). Broad excitation does the opposite: it amplifies "everything on".

Why it matters

  • The obvious candidate fails as drawn. With every connection excitatory, PFR is a relay: it hands travel direction (rotated by a fixed angle) to the steering neurons and the lateral accessory lobe. That is a real function, but it is not memory.
  • The gap is one bit of information. If the broad PFR recurrence is inhibitory, either because PFR_a is glutamatergic or because an inhibitory tangential input (FB3C, GABA, targets PFR_b) supplies it, the same wiring becomes an integrator. Nothing else in the fan-shaped body has both a travel-direction input and column-matched recurrence.
  • It explains the drift. A relay with a fixed offset predicts exactly what Flores-Valle saw: PFR follows walking direction, and when velocity input stops the population settles wherever its recurrence pushes it. An integrator would predict the opposite: PFR should hold its value when the fly stops.

What would settle it

  • Immunostain PFR_a for glutamate vs acetylcholine (VGlut / ChAT). This is the decisive measurement.
  • Two-colour imaging of hΔB and PFR: a relay predicts a fixed ~90–135° phase offset between the two bumps; an integrator predicts PFR lagging hΔB after every turn and holding when the fly stops.
  • Image PFR during Behbahani's re-zeroing task: an integrator's amplitude should fall to zero at the fictive food centre.
