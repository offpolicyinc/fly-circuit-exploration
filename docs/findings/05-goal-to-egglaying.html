The navigation goal has a direct, two-way line to the egg-laying circuit

Nobody was looking for this. An unbiased scan of where central-complex neurons send their output outside the navigation system found that the goal population itself, and three fan-shaped-body output types that read it, converge on oviIN, the inhibitory neuron that gates egg laying. Roughly 3,500 synapses in total. And oviIN sends back into the goal population and its tangential inputs. The fly's "where am I trying to go" signal and its "should I lay an egg here" signal are wired to each other, and no navigation study mentions it.

Background

Goal population
FC2 neurons (three subtypes) hold the direction the fly is trying to go; steering neurons compare it with heading (Mussells Pires 2024).
FS neurons
Fan-shaped-body output cells that read columnar activity and project out of the central complex to higher brain areas. FS1A (three subtypes, 47 cells) reads the goal layer. No function published.
oviIN
A single pair of inhibitory neurons in the female egg-laying circuit. It suppresses the descending neurons (oviDNs) that trigger egg deposition; its activity is one of the switches that decides whether a female lays here or moves on (Wang et al. 2020).
Prior work (novelty sweep, 2026-09-12)
Weber-Langstaff, Srivastava, Kunin & Gutierrez 2025 (eNeuro) report from the hemibrain that FS1A is the strongest input to oviIN and that FC2B/FC2C join it in a recurrent module. The forward connection is therefore published. New here: MaleCNS replication (and hemibrain FS1A→oviIN 1,589 synapses), oviIN's return projections into FC2C and the FB5 tangentials, and the navigation-state reading.
Caveat
MaleCNS is a male brain. The cell annotated oviIN is the male homolog; the connections are real, their behavioural meaning is inferred from the female literature and must be checked in a female connectome (FlyWire) and in behaviour.

Why it matters

  • A navigation state gates a reproductive decision. Egg-site selection is a spatial choice: females walk, sample, leave and return. A direct line from the goal representation to the egg-laying gate is the obvious way to couple "I have a destination" to "do not lay yet", or the reverse.
  • It runs both ways. oviIN's projections back into FC2C and the FB5 tangentials mean the egg-laying state can also alter the goal. Reproductive state modulating navigation is expected in principle; here is the wire.
  • It reframes the FS neurons. FS types are ~200 cells with no assigned function. FS1A's largest output being a single identified behavioural switch suggests each FS type may broadcast a specific navigation variable to a specific decision circuit.

What would settle it

  • Confirm the same connections in the female FlyWire connectome.
  • Silence FS1A in mated females and score egg-laying site choice and the walk-sample-return pattern.
  • Image FS1A during goal-directed walking (menotaxis) versus egg-laying bouts; predict activity tied to goal expression and suppressed during deposition.
