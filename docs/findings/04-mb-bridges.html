The learning centre and the navigation system are wired together, three ways

Flies have two separate systems that navigation research has kept apart: the mushroom body, which learns what is good and bad, and the central complex, which computes headings and vectors. A 2024 experiment showed flies need both to learn a place, with no known connection between them. The wiring diagram has three. Two carry learned value from the mushroom body into the travel-direction and steering populations; one carries "which way I have been going" back to the mushroom body's dopamine system. None of the bridge neurons has been recorded.

Background

Mushroom body (MB)
The fly's associative-learning centre. Odors and other cues are represented sparsely; dopamine neurons (PAM, PPL1) carry reward and punishment and adjust the synapses onto MB output neurons (MBONs), whose activity therefore reports learned value (Hige 2015).
Central complex (CX)
The navigation system: compass, velocity vectors, travel direction (hΔB), goal and steering (PFL3). Its fan-shaped body also contains ~160 types of tangential neurons (FBxx) that span all columns and mostly have no known function.
The experiment that needs a bridge
Chen et al. 2024: in a featureless arena, flies learn to return to a rewarded spot by marking it with their own scent, and the learning needs both the mushroom body (to value the scent) and PFNd (the self-motion input to hΔB). The authors had no circuit connecting the two.
Data
MaleCNS 2026 whole-brain connectome; every CX cell type profiled for inputs and outputs from outside the CX.

What we found

Measured, unbiased screen

Screening all CX types for external inputs flagged the ones fed by MBONs. Three routes connect the systems:

RoutePathKey numbers
1 · value → travel directionMBON21 + MBON09 → FB4R (6 glutamatergic cells) → hΔBFB4R gets 18 % of its input from the two MBONs; sends 3,647 synapses to hΔB, 8 % of hΔB's total input
2 · value → steeringMBON25 + MBON34 → FB4G, FB4H → vΔK, vΔM → hΔA → PFL3hΔA gives PFL3 3,881 synapses, more than any single goal (FC2) subtype
3 · travel direction → learninghΔB → FR1 → MBON30, PPL1 dopamine (PPL102), FB dopamine (FB5H)FR1 is 30 % self-recurrent (8,694 synapses), the strongest self-loop in the fan-shaped body

Glutamate is usually inhibitory in the fly, so routes 1 and 2 most likely suppress rather than drive their targets. Signs are predictions.

Why it matters

  • Learned value can bias where the fly thinks it is going. Route 1 lets an MB verdict ("this smells like the rewarded place") modulate hΔB, the population that represents travel direction. That is a mechanism for scent-marked place learning without a map, and it is the missing piece of Chen 2024.
  • Learned value can bias steering without touching the goal. Route 2 reaches PFL3 through hΔA, bypassing the FC2 goal population entirely. Value-driven turning and goal-driven turning would then be separate channels that sum at the same steering neurons.
  • The navigation system can teach the mushroom body. Route 3 sends a persistent copy of recent travel direction to the dopamine cells that write MB memories. That is what you would need to associate an odor or a scent mark with the direction you were moving when you met it.

What would settle it

  • Activate MBON21 or MBON09 while imaging hΔB: route 1 predicts a change in hΔB bump amplitude or offset, likely suppression.
  • Silence FR1 in Chen 2024's scent-marking task: route 3 predicts loss of the PFNd-dependent component of learning while odor learning itself is spared.
  • Image FR1 during walking: predict a slowly changing, direction-tuned signal that outlasts turns.
