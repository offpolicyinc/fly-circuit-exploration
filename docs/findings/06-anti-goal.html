Finding 6 of 8 · a hidden sign flip

The steering neurons receive the goal twice: once as is, once flipped by 180°

Steering works by comparing heading with a goal direction held by the FC2 population. FC2 delivers that goal straight to the steering neurons. The wiring shows a second, parallel route: FC2 also drives a set of eight neurons, hΔM, whose anatomy inverts any signal by half a turn, and hΔM delivers that inverted goal to the same steering neurons with almost as many synapses. The fly's steering system is wired to receive "toward the goal" and "away from the goal" at the same time, with an inhibitory gate deciding the mix.

Background

Goal and steering
FC2 neurons hold the goal as a bump in one of eight fan-shaped-body columns (each column is 45° of heading). PFL3 neurons receive the goal and the compass heading and fire when the two disagree, driving a corrective turn; PFL2 fire most when the fly faces directly away from the goal and add turning strength (Mussells Pires 2024; Westeinde 2024).
hΔ neurons
A family of fan-shaped-body cells with input in one column and output in the column half a turn away. Anatomically, every hΔ type inverts whatever vector it carries (Hulse 2021). Which signal each one inverts, and where it delivers it, is what the wiring can tell us.
Why an inverted goal is useful
Sometimes the task is to move away: flies learn to avoid a heading that brought heat (Dan 2024). The same goal memory could serve both approach and avoidance if something can flip its sign on demand.

Why it matters

  • PFL2's anti-goal tuning may come from here. Westeinde 2024 explained PFL2's preference for facing away from the goal by PFL2's own anatomy. hΔM supplies exactly that signal, pre-inverted, with 1,555 synapses. The two explanations make different predictions when hΔM is silenced.
  • A valence switch without a second memory. Approach uses the goal; avoidance uses the goal plus 180°. If FB5A's inhibition sets the balance between the direct and the hΔM route, one stored direction serves both, which is what Dan 2024's fast avoidance learning needs.
  • It is a family trait. hΔD, hΔE, hΔG and hΔH show the same 180° delivery into FC2 and PFL3 for other inputs, so "invert then deliver to the goal or steering layer" is a general fan-shaped-body operation, not a one-off.

What would settle it

  • Image hΔM alongside FC2: the wiring predicts an hΔM bump antiphase to the FC2 bump.
  • Silence hΔM during approach menotaxis versus heat-avoidance: predict avoidance impaired, approach intact, and a reduced PFL2 anti-goal signal.
  • Activate FB5A and measure the FC2 bump: predict narrowing or suppression and a change in the PFL2/PFL3 balance.
