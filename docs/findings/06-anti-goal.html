Finding 6 of 9 · re-ranked upward: the candidate return path

The steering neurons receive the goal twice: once as is, once flipped by 180°. The steering model uses only the first.

Steering works by comparing heading with a goal direction held by the FC2 population, which delivers the goal straight to the steering neurons. That is the published model. The wiring shows a parallel route of about half the size: FC2 also drives hΔM, a type whose anatomy inverts any signal by half a turn, and hΔM delivers the inverted goal to the same steering neurons. None of the wiring is new; it is in the 2021 hemibrain tables. What is new is stating its size relative to the direct route and asking what a steering neuron does with a goal and its opposite at the same time.

Background

Goal and steering
FC2 neurons hold the goal as a bump in one of eight fan-shaped-body columns (each column is 45° of heading). PFL3 neurons receive the goal and the compass heading and fire when the two disagree, driving a corrective turn; PFL2 fire most when the fly faces directly away from the goal and add turning strength (Mussells Pires 2024; Westeinde 2024).
hΔ neurons
A family of fan-shaped-body cells with input in one column and output in the column half a turn away. Anatomically, every hΔ type inverts whatever vector it carries (Hulse 2021). Which signal each one inverts, and where it delivers it, is what the wiring can tell us.
What Hulse et al. 2021 already said
That hΔ types invert by ~180° by construction, and, in one sentence, that PFL2/3 "receive shared input from a handful of hΔ types, several FC2 types, one or two vΔ types, and many FB tangential neuron types." No hΔ type was singled out and no strength was given.
Why an inverted goal is useful
Sometimes the task is to move away: flies learn to avoid a heading that brought heat (Dan 2024). The same goal memory could serve both approach and avoidance if something can flip its sign on demand.

FB5A, four GABAergic cells, inhibits FC2, PFL3 and hΔM together; it is a candidate global inhibitor of this stage, not a selector between the two routes.

Update, 2026-09-12: why this route may be the return path

Anchoring every fan-shaped-body phase to two published conventions (hΔ labels are dendritic columns, Hulse 2021; the hΔB travel bump sits on its axon, Lyu 2022) shows that all the hΔ routes that could store a displacement (hΔA, hΔH, hΔG, hΔI) deliver it to FC2 or PFL3 with its sign preserved, i.e. as the outbound direction. The only inverters downstream of the goal are hΔM (FC2 → hΔM → PFL3 at 178°) and the hΔK/PFGs loop. A closed-loop model shows return is impossible without a net inversion. So a stored displacement read directly makes the fly continue, and read through hΔM makes it return. Details: exhaustive-search.md §4. Prior work found in the sweep: Liao et al. 2025 describe a separate anti-goal circuit in the lateral accessory lobe; Nanni & Lee 2026 propose FB5A as the FC2 normaliser. The hΔM route itself is not in print.

Why it matters

  • The steering model is incomplete by a known amount. "PFL3 = heading versus goal" omits a second goal-derived input worth about half the first, of opposite sign in angle. A model that fits PFL3 tuning without it is fitting with the wrong inputs.
  • PFL2's anti-goal tuning has a candidate source. Westeinde 2024 explained PFL2's preference for facing away from the goal by PFL2's own anatomy. hΔM supplies that signal, pre-inverted, with 1,555 synapses. The two explanations diverge if hΔM is silenced.
  • A sign flip is what avoidance needs. If the same stored direction must serve approach and avoidance (Dan 2024), an inverted copy is the minimal machinery. Whether the fly uses hΔM that way is untested; the wiring only shows the copy exists.

What would settle it

  • Image hΔM alongside FC2: the wiring predicts an hΔM bump antiphase to the FC2 bump.
  • Silence hΔM during approach menotaxis versus heat-avoidance: predict avoidance impaired, approach intact, and a reduced PFL2 anti-goal signal.
  • Activate FB5A and measure the FC2 bump: predict global suppression of the goal layer (this tests the inhibitor reading, not route selection).
