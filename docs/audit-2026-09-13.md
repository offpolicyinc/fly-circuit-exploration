# Audit of the four findings, and seven new whole-brain screens

Date 2026-09-13. This pass re-derived every quantitative claim behind findings 1–4 from the raw MaleCNS v1.0 graph and the hemibrain v1.2 export, ran positive controls the original analysis lacked, and then ran unbiased screens over the whole brain rather than over the central complex alone. New scripts: `scripts/recurrence_modes_v2.py`, `scripts/brain_type_edges.py`, `scripts/hemibrain_type_edges.py`, `scripts/type_annotations.py`, `scripts/cx_ext_cell_edges.py`, `scripts/full_brain_screens.py`; simulations: `simulations/closed_loop_routes_tspace.py`, `simulations/eb_epg_pen_loop_variants.py`. New tables: `data/derived/brain_type_edges.csv.gz` (whole MaleCNS graph at cell-type level, 3.85 M type pairs), `hemibrain_type_edges.csv.gz`, `type_annotations.csv`, `cx_ext_cell_edges.csv`, `recurrence_modes_v2.csv`, `recurrence_loops_v2.csv`, `screen_*.csv`.

## 1. Finding 1: the synaptic store

### 1.1 The exclusion of activity-based storage does not hold

The original argument (`recurrence_modes.py`) scored each columnar type's within-type synapses as a fraction of its total input, took the cosine mode, and concluded that no population comes within an order of magnitude of the gain an integrator needs. Two problems.

First, a bug. The script mapped column labels with `c-1` then binned offsets modulo 8. For the 12-column hΔ types (A, B, C, I, J, K, L) and the 10-column vΔ types, column 12 minus column 1 became offset 3 instead of 180°, so the cosine gains in `recurrence_modes.csv` are wrong for exactly the types the finding is about. `recurrence_modes_v2.py` recomputes everything in angle space with the true column count per type.

Second, and decisive, a positive control. The compass ring attractor is the one recurrent network in this system whose persistent activity is measured. Scoring it with the same metric:

| edge | synapses | uniform gain | cosine gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| EPG → EPG | 11,486 | 0.056 | 0.050 |
| EPG → PEN_a | 6,100 | 0.141 | 0.079 |
| PEN_a → EPG | 14,398 | 0.070 | 0.034 |
| EPG ↔ PEN_a two-hop loop | | 0.0099 | 0.0027 |
| EPG ↔ PEN_b two-hop loop | | 0.0068 | 0.0022 |
| EPG → PEG → PEN_b → EPG | | | 0.0002 |

Corrected fan-shaped-body values by the same metric: vΔA_a self cosine gain 0.145, vΔI_b 0.060, vΔA_b 0.050, PFNv 0.046 (at 173°), the hΔ types 0.01–0.02 each with a 180° phase (a two-cell loop that preserves the column). Two-hop loops: vΔI_a ↔ vΔI_b 0.0024, hΔD ↔ vΔB 0.0012, hΔD ↔ vΔA_b 0.0010. So by this metric several fan-shaped-body populations recur more strongly than the compass, and the strongest fan-shaped-body loops sit within a factor of one to two of the compass loop. Synapse-count shares are not gains, and the metric cannot tell an integrator from a relay. The claim "activity storage ruled out for every population" is withdrawn. What survives is weaker: nothing in the fan-shaped body has conspicuously more recurrence than the compass, and the case for synaptic storage has to rest on positive evidence (the Gorko 2026 cAMP result, the write and reset coverage below), not on exclusion.

### 1.2 The column and arbor conventions hold up

The sign analysis depends on two conventions: hΔ neurons are named by their dendritic column (Hulse 2021, checked in the text), and the physiological travel bump sits on hΔB's axon (Lyu 2022). Checks:

- Every key kernel replicates in the hemibrain with the same labels: PFNd→hΔB 0.46 at 180° and 0.30 at 0° (MaleCNS 0.47 / 0.30); hΔB→hΔH 0.79 at 0° (0.82); hΔB→hΔA 0.65 (0.64); FC2B→hΔM 0.47 at 0° and 0.37 at −45° (0.38 / 0.41); hΔM→PFL3 0.52 / 0.44 at the two 180° bins (0.50 / 0.47); hΔA→PFL3, hΔC→FC2B, hΔB→PFR_a likewise.
- At cell resolution every one of the 19 hΔB cells receives PFNd on both arbors, 60 % on the axonal arbor, from both PFNd hemispheres alike, while PFNv is dendritic only. Lyu 2022 saw the same in the hemibrain and modelled it as a subtraction (axonal minus dendritic contribution, e.g. 257 − 164 = 93). The repository's treatment of hΔJ (bimodal input cancels) is the same rule, applied consistently.
- With the bump on the axon, hΔB→hΔH/hΔA/hΔI/hΔG contacts are axo-axonal in the same column, so those cells relay the travel direction unrotated. This stands.

Consequences. The sentence "two dopamine cell types, FB4M and FB1H, contact every cell of all four types across all columns" is wrong. FB4M covers hΔA and hΔI completely and hΔH and hΔG barely; FB1H covers hΔJ completely and every other candidate only in part. hΔH's uniform dopamine input is FB5H, a type the finding never mentioned, whose own inputs are vΔ types, CRE and SMP neurons, FR1 and FB5A, not the velocity population. The largest modulatory input to hΔA and hΔI is serotonergic, FB4Y, which is driven by the compass (PEN_a 7 %, EPG 5 %, ExR7 5 %, ExR5 4 %). The reset candidate OA-VPM3 is uniform on hΔH, hΔG, hΔM and hΔB, patchy on hΔA, absent on hΔI.

The drivers of the gates were also overstated. FB4M's velocity inputs (PFNv 6 %, hΔB 4 %, PFNd 3 %) are 13 % of its input; its largest single source is SMP377 (6 %), and it receives an ascending neuron, AN19B019, directly (385 synapses) and through PLP246 (660). FB1H's largest input is hΔJ (10 %), not the velocity neurons. OA-VPM3's Kenyon-cell input is 4 % (KCg-m 801 synapses); its largest is SMP457 (8 %).

Ranking after correction: hΔA has the strongest case (uniform FB4M dopamine, uniform FB4Y serotonin, a partial OA-VPM3 reset, 3,881 synapses to PFL3); hΔH second (uniform OA-VPM3 reset and uniform FB5H dopamine, the cleanest column matching, output to FC2B and PFL3); hΔI has a write gate but no reset; hΔG has a reset but no write gate.

### 1.4 The simulations were run in the wrong frame

`closed_loop_sites.py` and `site_readout_error.py` write the travel bump at hΔB's label column and read it out through label-space kernels. Section 4 of `exhaustive-search.md` anchors the bump on the axon, 180° from the label. The two are not the same: in label space the direct hΔA→PFL3 route returns (10 %, closest 3.0) and the FC2→hΔM route walks away; in the axon frame it is the reverse. The text quotes the label-space stored-length ratios (sign-independent, still valid) and the kernel-control rotations (explicit, still valid), so no published number is wrong, but `closed_loop_sites_results.json` and `site_readout_error.json` should not be read as support for the sign argument.

`closed_loop_routes_tspace.py` reruns every route with the bump written at label + 180° and PFL3 columns in the base frame:

| route | open-loop readout error | returned | closest | label-space error (old frame) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| hΔA → PFL3 direct | 148° | 0 % | 11.1 | 32° |
| hΔA → hΔI (dendrite) → PFL3 | 34° | 12 % | 3.3 | 146° |
| hΔA direct + via hΔI, weighted 3,881 : 1,388 | 148° | 0 % | 11.1 | 32° |
| hΔI → PFL3 direct | 147° | 0 % | 11.1 | 33° |
| hΔH → FC2B → PFL3 direct | 145° | 5 % | 11.1 | 35° |
| hΔH → FC2B → hΔM → PFL3 | 34° | 12 % | 3.6 | 146° |
| hΔH → FC2B → {direct + via hΔM}, weighted 2,478 : 1,073 | 144° | 12 % | 11.1 | 36° |
| hΔJ → FC2A → PFL3 direct | 146° | 5 % | 11.1 | 34° |
| hΔG → FC2C → PFL3 direct | 142° | 10 % | 11.1 | 38° |

Both inverting routes give a readout within 34° of home; every direct route points away. The return rates with this controller are low (12 %) because the controller is crude, so the readout error is the number to use. The synapse-weighted sums are dominated by the direct routes, which is the balance problem the finding already assigns to FB5A.

## 2. Finding 2: the inverter

### 2.1 hΔM is not the only one

An exhaustive screen of every two-hop columnar route from each store candidate or FC2 type to PFL3, PFL2 and PFL1 (both legs ≥ 150 synapses, rotation from `fb_offset_stats.csv`), expressed relative to the travel bump on the hΔB axon:

| route | synapses (leg 1, leg 2) | net rotation relative to T | reaches |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| hΔA → PFL3 | 3,881 | +14° | keep going |
| hΔI → PFL3 | 3,468 | +12° | keep going |
| hΔH → FC2B → PFL3 | 2,437, 2,478 | +23° | keep going |
| hΔJ → FC2A → PFL3 | 3,062, 2,512 | +13° | keep going |
| **hΔA → hΔI → PFL3** | **1,388, 3,468** | **+195°** | **go back** |
| **hΔA → hΔI → PFL2** | **1,388, 1,942** | **+197°** | **go back** |
| FC2B → hΔM → PFL3 | 1,073, 2,428 | 178° from FC2 | go back |
| FC2C → hΔM → PFL3 | 665, 2,428 | 176° | go back |
| FC2A → hΔA → PFL3 | 154, 3,881 | 179° | go back (weak) |
| PFR_a → hΔI → PFL3 | 394, 3,468 | 174° | go back (concentration 0.29) |

hΔA's axon lands on hΔI's dendritic arbor: in MaleCNS every one of the 17 hΔI cells receives hΔA at the opposite column (per-cell same : opposite = 5 : 96, 7 : 116, 3 : 63 …); in the hemibrain 63 % of 1,170 synapses sit at 180°. hΔI's axon then delivers that signal to PFL3 and PFL2 half a turn from where the direct hΔA→PFL3 route delivers it. hΔI therefore carries two copies of the travel signal with opposite sign: the current step from hΔB on its axon (2,842 synapses, unrotated) and hΔA's output on its dendrite (1,296, rotated). If hΔA is a store, hΔI's output is the current step minus the stored displacement, which is a comparison, not a relay. That is a hypothesis; what is established is that hΔM is the only inverter downstream of FC2, and hΔA → hΔI is a second inverter upstream of it, both converging on PFL3 and PFL2.

Also new: the 180° self-connections hΔI→hΔI (548 synapses, 179°), hΔA→hΔA (284, 180°) and hΔI→hΔA (245) are axon-to-dendrite contacts between cells half a turn apart, i.e. two-cell loops that return the bump to its own column. They are the fan-shaped-body analogue of the compass loop and score at the same order by the recurrence metric (section 1.1).

### 2.2 What stands

The FC2 → hΔM → PFL3/PFL2 route, its 178° rotation, its hemibrain replication and the PFL2 interpretation all stand. "The only thing that turns it around" becomes "one of two".

## 3. Finding 3: velocity sources

- **PS196 → GLNO is in print.** Hulse, Stanoev, Turner-Evans, Seelig and Jayaraman 2023 (bioRxiv 10.1101/2023.09.25.559373) identify PS196 as the largest input to GLNO, list LAL139 and LAL184 as the other LAL inputs, and propose that GLNO inherits its rotational-velocity estimate from a single upstream type. The MaleCNS fan-out (PS196_b → LPsP 942, ExR2 899, ExR4 686, FB3A 195) is the part not in that paper.
- **PS196_b is not obviously an efference copy.** Its inputs are PS099_a/b (18 %), PS048_a, PS047_b, the ascending neuron AN07B037_a (7 %), PS262, PS239, PS292, ExR8 (3 %) and OA-VUMa1; no descending neuron appears. Its largest output is untyped (69 %), then GLNO, OA-VUMa1 (1,055), LPsP, ExR2, ExR4. An ascending input is more consistent with a leg- or body-derived turning signal than with a motor command copy. Under the merged name PS196 the hemibrain replicates PS196 → GLNO (531), PS196 → LNO2 (275), LAL139 → GLNO (121) and LAL184 → GLNO (114); AN07B037 and PS326 have no typed counterpart there, so the ascending leg of the route cannot be checked in the female.
- **FB3A is multimodal.** Inputs: PFNd 34 %, hΔB 7 %, LAL143 6 %, PS326 4 %, FB4M 4 %, LNO1 4 %, AN06B009 3 %, PS196_b 2 %. LAL143's top input is LLPC1 (14 %), a lobula-plate columnar type carrying visual motion; PS326's top inputs are JO-EV3 and JO-EV5 (Johnston's organ, antennal mechanosensation) and the ascending neurons AN07B004 and AN19B024. So FB3A pools optic flow, antennal airflow or gravity cues and leg-derived signals before feeding PFNd. It remains the only large PFNd input with a direct ascending contact, but "the leg-derived speed estimate" overstates it.
- **Unreported observation.** GLNO receives PEN_a (25 %) and PEN_b (12 %) back from the shifters it drives (GLNO → PEN 82 % of its output). The rotational-velocity input to the compass is reciprocal with its target.

## 4. Finding 4: the compass brake

`eb_epg_pen_loop_variants.py` re-implements the write-tile contact three ways at the measured 2.7 : 1 ratio (rotation gain with the contact absent is 139°/s):

| implementation | dark bump | rotation gain at 1× | at 2.7× |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| somatic drive added to PEN input (original) | 0.79 | 0°/s | 0°/s |
| presynaptic release gain, total release held fixed (pure redistribution) | 0.91 | −25°/s | 0°/s |
| somatic drive with divisive normalisation of the PEN population | 0.98 | −34°/s | 0°/s |

The brake survives every implementation, including the one that treats the contact as axo-axonic modulation of release (the second of the finding's three escape routes). That leaves functional weakness of the synapses or unmodelled inhibition. No change to the finding; the model claim is stronger than before.

## 5. New unbiased screens

All from the whole-brain type graph unless stated. Tables in `data/derived/screen_*.csv`; printed summaries in `scripts/full_brain_screens.py`.

**S1 External inputs to central-complex types.** Of 263 k synapses from typed non-CX neurons into CX types, the largest shares are PS196_b → GLNO (19 %), CB1617 → FB1D (17 %), LAL156_b → LNOa (15 %), PS263 → SpsP (15 %), PS326 → SpsP (12 %), GNG311 → IbSpsP (11 %), MBON21 → FB4R (11 %, replicates in the hemibrain at 13 %). Ascending neurons contribute 2.4 k synapses, mostly to IbSpsP, FB4M, FB3A and ExR3; descending neurons 1.6 k, mostly to FB8B, ExR6, LNO2 and FB8C. The velocity inputs to the noduli and bridge (LNO1, LNO2, LNOa, SpsP, GLNO, LPsP) are fed by LAL and posterior-slope interneurons; some replicate in the hemibrain under merged names (PS196 → GLNO 531, LAL143 → FB3A 195, LAL139 and LAL184 → GLNO), while PS326, AN07B037 and the LNOa/SpsP inputs LAL156 and PS263 are either untyped or unconnected there, which may be typing or the hemibrain's incomplete posterior slope.

**S2 Neuromodulation.** Dopamine enters the fan-shaped body through seven tangential types (FB4M 14 k synapses onto CX types, FB1H 9 k, FB1C 7 k, FB2A 6 k, ExR2 6 k, FB5H 5 k, FB4L 3 k); the PPL and PAM clusters contribute under 300 synapses each. Octopamine is EL (30 k, ring neurons) and OA-VPM3 (8 k). Serotonin is ExR3 (16 k, onto ER3d rings, hΔK 7.8 % and PFGs 5.5 %) and FB4Y (12 k, onto hΔA 3.1 %, vΔK 8.3 %, hΔI 2.5 %). FB4Y is the largest modulatory input to two of the store candidates and is compass-driven; no finding mentions it.

**S3 Sex.** The annotation table marks hΔA, hΔB, hΔI, hΔJ, hΔL, hΔM and the FC1/FC3 types `fru_low`; no CX-connected type is annotated as structurally dimorphic apart from DNp48 ("potentially"). Comparing every CX edge with ≥ 400 synapses between MaleCNS and the hemibrain after merging subtype suffixes, 86 % of 1,967 edges agree within a factor of two (median |log2 ratio| 0.35). The large disagreements are LAL, PS and WED inputs to the noduli, bridge and rings that are absent in the female table (some of those types are untyped there, others are typed but unconnected, which may reflect the hemibrain's truncated posterior slope), and "female-enriched" rows that are untyped-source pools. There is no evidence here for sexually dimorphic central-complex wiring, and no power to find subtle differences.

**S4 Descending targets.** Descending neurons with ≥ 3 % of their input from CX types: DNg36_a (18 %, all from ExR8), DNpe016 (7 %, PFL3, 457 synapses), DNa03 (6 %, PFL2 1,571 and PFL3 468; known), DNb01 (5 %, PFL3 753), DNp48 (4 %, FB5G and FS3), DNc02 (4 %), DNbe006 (4 %, PFL3). ExR8 → DNg36_a is the largest single CX-to-descending share in the brain; ExR8 itself is driven by PEN_b (23 %), EPG (16 %), ExR4 and GLNO, so it is a compass-derived output to a descending neuron that no navigation paper names. PFL3 → DNpe016 and PFL3 → DNb01 are additional steering outputs beside DNa02/DNa03.

**S5 Ascending routes.** Direct: AN19B019 → FB4M (385), AN06B009 → FB3A (319), AN07B004 → ExR3 (216), AN07B037 → IbSpsP. Two-hop, ranked by the weaker leg: AN19B019 → PLP246 → FB4M (660), AN07B037 → PS196_a → LNO2 (550), AN07B004 → PS326 → FB3A (511) and → SpsP (431), AN07B037_a → PS196_b → GLNO (419), → LPsP, → ExR2, → ExR4. So the ascending neuron AN07B037 stands upstream of both velocity systems (GLNO for rotation, LNO2 for translation), AN07B004 feeds the translational side through PS326, and AN19B019 feeds the dopamine type FB4M. AN19B019's own inputs are descending neurons (DNp54 11 %, DNae009, DNg02) and AN07B004, so it is a leg-circuit relay that returns a descending command's consequences to the fan-shaped body's write gate. These are the strongest structural candidates for "the fly is walking" and "the fly is turning" signals reaching the vector system.

**S6 Column shifts (null).** Among 1,000+ columnar pairs with ≥ 300 synapses and concentration ≥ 0.6, every mean offset is within 25° of 0° or 180°, except pairs involving the six-column hΔF, the five-labelled vΔH and FC2 → hΔM (−24°, an eight-versus-nine column mismatch). There is no fan-shaped-body shifter beyond the known PFN hemispheric ±45° offsets.

**S7 Column-specific tangential input (null).** For every tangential type with ≥ 300 synapses onto a columnar type, the resultant length of its synapses across the target's columns has median 0.09. The only values above 0.5 are bridge neurons that are glomerulus-specific by construction (EPGt → PFNd, P6-8P9 → PFNa) and dorsal-layer inputs to vΔA_a and FS4A (FB9A, FB9C, FB5K). Onto FC2, PFL3 and the store candidates the maximum is 0.28, and the mild bias toward column 0 there is an artefact of mapping C1 and C9 to the same angle. No tangential neuron sets a column in the goal or steering layer; goals must enter through columnar routes or through uniform gating of column-specific plastic synapses.

## 6. What was changed in the findings

Audit notes were added at the top of drafts 1, 2 and 3 (and rebuilt into the HTML pages) pointing here. The prose of the findings was left as written so that the record of what was claimed on 2026-09-12 stays legible; this document is the correction.

## 7. What would be worth doing next

1. Image hΔI together with hΔA and hΔB during a return: if hΔI is a comparator its bump should sit between the current-step and stored directions and shrink as they align.
2. Test FB4Y (serotonin) and FB5H (dopamine) as the modulators of hΔA/hΔI and hΔH respectively; the finding's FB4M/FB1H prediction only applies to hΔA, hΔI and hΔJ.
3. Record AN07B037 and AN19B019 during walking: predicted turning and walking signals reaching PS196/GLNO and FB4M.
4. Replace the recurrence metric with a per-cell effective-weight model (synapse count normalised by target dendritic size, with transmitter sign) and fit it to the compass so that the same fit can be applied to the fan-shaped body loops.
