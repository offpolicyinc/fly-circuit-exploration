# Kapoor & Waddell 2024: OA-VPM3 gates recent-memory expression

From the abstract: the two OA-VPM3 neurons provide most octopamine input to the mushroom-body network. Activating them during learning enhances short-term memory and can divert odour salience; adrenergic-like receptor knock-downs in specific MBONs alter STM; OA-VPM3 activation temporarily blocks long-term memory expression. Octopamine reconfigures the MBON network to prioritise recent over remote memories. Reported by S. Thornquist: silencing OA-VPM3 does not affect appetitive memory and OA-VPM3 activation does not substitute for reward (Fig. 1E, F).

Bearing on this repository: earlier text called OA-VPM3 a reward neuron and octopamine the fly's reward transmitter, on the basis of the older octopamine-and-sugar literature. That was wrong. OA-VPM3 is now described by its measured function and kept as a wiring-based reset candidate only.
